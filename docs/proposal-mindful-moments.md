# Mindful Moments with Amake Fe

**A community platform — and a way for the community to support the work.**

---

## The position

A Facebook page with **more than 200,000 followers**. Years of real marriage and family
stories, collected by phone and voice note, written by one person, published to an audience
that keeps coming back.

And no way for any of that effort to be covered. **Facebook's monetisation programmes are
not open to creators in Zambia** — no in-stream ads, no content bonuses, no subscriptions.
The reach is real; there is no route back from it, and every hour behind it is absorbed by
the person doing the work.

**The audience is already built. What is missing is a way for it to give something back.**

## The proposal

Two applications and a payment rail:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The reading app** | Where the community reads, follows a series, comments, votes and supports |
| **The studio** | Where Amake Fe writes, schedules, publishes and posts to Facebook |
| **Mobile money** | MTN MoMo, Airtel Money and Zamtel Kwacha, collected directly in the app |

## 1. Facebook becomes the front door, not the destination

Facebook keeps doing what it does best: putting a story in front of 200,000 people. Every
release still goes out as a post, with its picture and its hook — and publishing to the app
and posting to Facebook are a **single action**, not a second errand.

What changes is where the story finishes: the post carries a link, and the story opens in
the app.

Facebook cannot tell her which part of a six-part series readers stop at. It cannot hold a
reader's place, cannot be searched, cannot notify anyone, and **cannot take a contribution**.
Those four gaps are the case for building something alongside it.

## 2. The apps turn an audience into a community

**Reading is free and requires no account** — a reader arriving from a Facebook post is
reading in one tap. That is deliberate: the funnel cannot afford a sign-up wall in front of
the story.

What the app adds on top of reach:

- **Serialised stories with the reader's place kept.** A six-part story brings one reader
  back six times. Retention is the thing Facebook does not provide.
- **A searchable archive by topic**, so four years of work keeps working instead of
  disappearing down a feed.
- **A community timeline** — the questions she answers, polls, notices — with comments and
  reactions, so the audience talks to her *and to each other* in a space she controls.
- **Measurement that matters**: reads per part, completion, saves, comments. Which topic
  brings people back, and how long the next series should run.

## 3. Community support: covering what the work costs

**This is not a business built on the audience.** It is a way for an audience to carry the
cost of something it already values.

The work is real and it is not free — the hours on the phone listening, the writing and
rewriting, airtime and data, and the running of the platform itself. All of it is currently
absorbed by one person. Community support is how that stops being true.

Readers contribute with mobile money, in a few taps — no card, no bank account, no app
store. **Every story stays free.** The ask appears only *after* something — at the end of a part,
under a post someone cared about — never in front of it. Nothing is gated, nothing is
withheld, and no reader is ever asked to pay to read.

**How it settles.** The platform collects and handles the payment rails, hosting and
operations, and retains **20%** to cover them. **80% goes to the creator**, settled with a
full statement — what came in, what was deducted, what was paid, and into which account.

What modest participation covers, at K30 a contribution:

| Contributions in a month | Collected | To the creator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | K3,000 | K2,400 |
| 250 | K7,500 | K6,000 |
| 500 | K15,000 | K12,000 |

500 contributions is **0.25% of the existing following**. The point is not the ceiling. It
is that a small, willing fraction of an audience this size is enough to cover the work
properly — without a single story going behind a paywall.

**Support is also measured.** Every contribution records *which ask produced it* — the story
ending, the community post, the home card — so the placement that works can be kept and the
one that intrudes can be removed. It records nothing about who gave.

## 4. Trust is the product, and it is enforced by the system

The reason people phone her in the first place is that their story will be handled safely.
That promise is the asset, and on this platform it is not a policy — it is built in:

- **Names are redacted automatically** everywhere a story appears, including the Facebook
  post. Not by remembering to do it.
- **Publishing is blocked** if a phone number or email is left in a story by accident.
- **No contributor data is stored at all** — no names, no numbers, no recordings. Those stay
  on her phone. There is nothing here to leak.
- **The party being paid does not record the payout.** Settlements run through a separate
  console with separate access, so every kwacha can be accounted for.

## 5. Where it grows

| | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Notifications** | The largest single lever on repeat readership — telling 200,000 people the moment a part lands, without paying for reach |
| **A public web archive** | Found by people who have never heard of the page, still reaching readers years after publication |
| **App Store and Play Store** | The same product, on the home screen |
| **Recurring support** | A standing monthly contribution, so the work is not funded month to month by chance |
| **Sponsored support** | A business underwriting a series, openly credited to the community |

Each builds on what exists. None requires starting again.

---

**In one line:** Facebook delivers the audience, the apps hold it, and community support
lets that audience carry the cost of the work — in a market where the platform itself
returns nothing.
